This is my signature walk, and the one closest to my work as an engineer. The Kathmandu Valley’s hiti — stone spouts, also called dhunge dhara — are not ruins. Many still carry water, part of a 1,500-year-old system of canals, ponds and groundwater that communities have looked after for generations.

I measure and draw these spouts in cross-section. Those drawings were cited in a TU Berlin master’s thesis (2023) and featured in Nepali Times, and I lead groundwater-recharge projects in Kathmandu and Lalitpur. On the walk I turn that into something you can see from the street: where the water comes from, how it travels, and why it still comes out of the spout.

I have led this walk for diplomats, researchers, water professionals and World Economic Forum Global Shapers, and I have been an academic field guide for Brunel University master’s (MSc) research. Tell me who is coming and what they want to understand, and I will plan the route around them.
