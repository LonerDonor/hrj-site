## The idea

The Kathmandu Valley’s water heritage is a whole system, and it makes most sense on foot: Rajkulo canals, pukhu ponds, hiti stone spouts, jaru water rituals and the guthi — <span class="deva" lang="ne">गुठी</span> — community institutions that organise their care. The water heritage walk follows that system from source to spout and explains how each part works.

## What I did

Since 2017 I have designed and guided heritage walks across Lalitpur and the Kathmandu Valley. I have led walks for researchers, diplomats, water professionals, the World Economic Forum’s Global Shapers and international organisations — including a group of technical water professionals with Prakriti Foundation — and I was an academic field guide for Brunel University MSc research.

## What came of it

More than 50 walks so far, on water, food and urban heritage. Each route is planned around what the group wants to understand, and the engineering is never far away: the spouts on the route are the kind of system I survey and draw in section.

The walks have their own pages, with the stops, the themes and how to book a walk for your group.
