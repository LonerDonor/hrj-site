## Why the spouts matter

Hiti — <span class="deva" lang="new">हिति</span>, also known in Nepali as dhunge dhara — are the stone water spouts of the Kathmandu Valley: a water system around 1,500 years old, and many of the spouts still run. They are usually treated as monuments. I treat them as working hydraulic infrastructure: gravity-fed systems in which groundwater, structure, community governance and ritual all depend on one another.

## What I did

From 2022 I carried out a pro bono field investigation of the internal hydraulic structure of the Valley’s hiti, and recorded what I found as computer-aided design (CAD) cross-sections — the part of a spout nobody sees from the courtyard. I was then technical contributor and illustrator for Nepali Times’ feature *Kathmandu’s ancient water spouts still functioning*, where my drawings and engineering analysis were the primary technical source.

## What came of it

The drawings have since been cited in a master’s thesis at Technische Universität Berlin (TU Berlin) in 2023, and republished by Rethinking the Future and Architexturez. The same understanding goes into practice: from June to December 2024 I was technical advisor to Kathmandu Metropolitan City (KMC) on two hiti reconstruction projects.

If you are restoring or rebuilding a spout, this is the groundwork I bring — how the system works below the paving, not only what it looks like above it.
